What this FD calculator shows

The maturity figure combines your original deposit with modeled compound interest. The effective annual yield converts the entered nominal rate and compounding frequency into a one-year effective rate.

Why frequency changes maturity

If the nominal annual rate is held constant, interest credited more frequently begins earning interest sooner. The difference is usually modest, but it becomes more visible with larger deposits and longer terms.

Use the sensitivity table properly

The lower and higher scenarios are not predictions or currently available FD quotes. They simply show how a one-percentage-point change would affect the same deposit and term.

Common questions

Does CalcKosh use the bank's exact FD method?

No. This is a planning model using the entered nominal annual rate and compounding frequency. Bank day-count, payout, rounding and product rules can differ.

Does the result include tax or TDS?

No. The maturity and interest figures shown here are before tax treatment applicable to you.

Why does compounding frequency matter?

With the same nominal annual rate, more frequent compounding produces a slightly higher effective annual yield.
